# RB-M365-004 Teams audio/video and meeting issues

## Purpose / When to use

Use when a user reports Teams problems: others cannot hear them, they cannot hear others, camera not working or black, echo, choppy or robotic audio, video freezing, screen share failing, or they cannot join meetings. Covers the new Teams client on Windows 11 and Teams on the web. Teams Phone (calling plans) is not standard for our clients; desk phones are Yealink on the client's VoIP provider (RB-VOIP-001).

## Prerequisites / access needed

- Beacon RMM remote session (note: remote control tools can interfere with audio during a call; test outside a meeting where possible)
- Teams admin center via GDAP (Teams Administrator) for call analytics
- The user, with their headset/camera, available for a test call

## Steps

### 1. Narrow it down (5 minutes)

1. Is it **one user** or **many**? Many users at one site with choppy audio/video usually means internet trouble (RB-NET-001) or Wi-Fi (RB-NET-002). Check https://status.cloud.microsoft and M365 admin center > Health > Service health for a Teams incident.
2. Is it **every meeting** or one meeting? A single external meeting (someone else's tenant or a webinar) may be the organizer's problem.
3. Does **Teams on the web** (https://teams.microsoft.com in Edge) behave the same? If the web works, the problem is the desktop client or local devices.

### 2. Test call

4. In Teams: **Settings (...) > Settings > Devices**. Select **Make a test call**. The test call records and plays back audio and shows which mic/speaker/camera was used and network status.

### 3. Audio problems

5. In Teams Settings > Devices, confirm the correct **Speaker**, **Microphone**, and **Audio device** are selected. A docking station, monitor speakers, and headset can all appear; users frequently end up on the monitor's speakers or the laptop mic.
6. Windows: **Settings > System > Sound**. Check input level moves when the user speaks. Check **Settings > Privacy & security > Microphone**: "Microphone access" and "Let apps access your microphone" must be on, and Microsoft Teams must be allowed.
7. Bluetooth headsets: Windows may switch to the hands-free profile with poor quality. Use the headset's USB dongle if it has one. Re-pair if it keeps dropping.
8. Echo: usually someone on speakers with an open mic. Have the user use a headset, or turn on noise suppression (Teams Settings > Devices > Noise suppression: Auto or High).
9. Update headset firmware and Dell audio drivers via **Dell Command | Update**.

### 4. Camera problems

10. Teams Settings > Devices > **Camera**: right camera selected, preview shows video.
11. Windows **Settings > Privacy & security > Camera**: access on, Teams allowed.
12. Dell Latitude privacy shutter: check that the physical shutter is open (black preview with the camera "working" is usually the shutter). Some models have a function key camera toggle.
13. Close other apps using the camera (Zoom, browser tabs, Windows Camera). Only one app can use it at a time.
14. Device Manager > Cameras: if the device shows an error, disable and enable, or update the driver.

### 5. Clear the Teams cache (new Teams)

15. Quit Teams completely (right-click the tray icon > Quit).
16. Delete the contents of:
    `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Packages\MSTeams_8wekyb3d8bbwe\LocalCache\Microsoft\MSTeams`
    Or: Settings > Apps > Installed apps > Microsoft Teams > Advanced options > **Reset**.
17. Restart Teams, sign in, test call again.

### 6. Screen share

18. Screen sharing on Windows needs no special permission, but some clients' DLP or older display drivers cause black shares. Update graphics drivers (Dell Command | Update). If the user has multiple monitors, have them share a window rather than a screen to test.
19. Presenter cannot share in an external meeting: the organizer's meeting options may restrict who can present. Nothing for us to fix.

### 7. Network quality

20. If the test call shows poor network, or call quality drops for several users: check whether they are on Wi-Fi far from an AP, on a guest network, or on VPN. Teams traffic should not go over the FortiClient tunnel; split tunneling is our standard (RB-NET-003).
21. Teams admin center > **Users > Manage users** > select user > **Meetings & calls** (call analytics). Look at the specific call's network section: jitter, packet loss, round-trip time. Packet loss over about 5% or jitter over 30 ms explains choppy audio.

## Priority guidance

- Single user, workaround (phone dial-in, web client, another device): **P3**.
- Single user with an important meeting starting in under an hour: still **P3**, but take it now if you can; VIP fully unable to meet is **P2**.
- Many users at a site with poor call quality: **P2**; check internet and Wi-Fi.

## Escalate if

- Call analytics shows consistent packet loss from a site: Tom Kessler (network).
- Teams sign-in loops or licensing errors: Renee Castillo.
- Conference room systems (Teams Rooms devices, room cameras): Tier 2, then Tom Kessler or the vendor.
- Hardware failure (dead headset, dead webcam on a laptop under warranty): RB-HW-003 or Dell ProSupport via Brian Novak.

## Ticket notes

- One user or many, desktop vs web result
- Devices selected in Teams and Windows, privacy settings checked
- Cache cleared/reset (yes/no), drivers updated
- Call analytics findings if checked (jitter, loss, RTT)
- Test call result and user confirmation
