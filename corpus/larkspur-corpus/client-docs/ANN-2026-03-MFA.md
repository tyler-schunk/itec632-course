# A Quick MFA Reminder: No Text Message Codes, and How Number Matching Works

**March 9, 2026**

Hello from Larkspur Ridge IT,

It has been about a year since we changed how new users sign up for multi-factor authentication (MFA), and we still get questions about it. Here's a friendly refresher you can share with your staff.

## The rule, in one sentence

**Since March 2025, we no longer accept text message (SMS) codes as an MFA method for new enrollments. Everyone enrolls with the Microsoft Authenticator app, using number matching.**

## A quick refresher: what is MFA?

MFA means it takes two things to sign in to your work account:
1. Something you **know** - your password
2. Something you **have** - your phone, with the Microsoft Authenticator app

Even if a criminal steals your password, they can't sign in without your phone.

## Why not text message codes?

Text codes were a big improvement over passwords alone, but criminals have gotten good at getting around them. They can:
- Trick a phone carrier into moving your number to their phone ("SIM swapping")
- Call you pretending to be IT and ask you to read the code back to them
- Send a fake sign-in page that captures the code as you type it

The Authenticator app with number matching is much harder to fool.

## What is number matching?

When you sign in to Microsoft 365 on your computer, here's what happens:

1. You type your email and password like normal.
2. **A two-digit number appears on your computer screen.** For example: **47**.
3. Your phone buzzes with a notification from Microsoft Authenticator asking, "Are you trying to sign in?"
4. **You type the number from your computer screen (47) into your phone** and tap **Yes**.
5. You're in!

That's it. It usually takes about five seconds once you get used to it.

### Why does it work this way?

The number proves that **you** are the one sitting at the computer that's trying to sign in. A criminal trying to sign in from somewhere else would see a different number on their screen, and you would have no way of knowing it. So if your phone ever asks you to enter a number and **you are not signing in right now, deny it**.

## The golden rules

1. **Only approve a sign-in you started yourself.** If your phone asks for a number and you didn't just try to sign in, tap **No, it's not me** or **Deny**.
2. **Never read a number or code to anyone.** Larkspur will never call you and ask for your MFA number. Neither will Microsoft. Anyone who asks is a scammer.
3. **Getting lots of prompts you didn't ask for?** Someone may have your password. Deny them all and **call us at (913) 555-0111** right away so we can change your password.
4. **Getting a new phone?** Call us *before* you trade in the old one if you can. We'll help you move Authenticator over.

## What about people who already use text codes?

A small number of people enrolled before March 2025 may still have text codes on their account. We've been moving them to the Authenticator app over time, usually when they get a new phone or need an MFA reset. If you'd like to switch now, submit a ticket and we'll help you in about 10 minutes.

## What if I lose my phone or can't sign in?

Call the support line at (913) 555-0111. To protect your account, an MFA reset takes two steps:

1. Our technician will **call you back at the phone number we have on file** for you.
2. Our technician will also **confirm the reset with your organization's authorized contact** (usually your office manager or owner).

We never verify your identity by date of birth or Social Security number. This might take a few extra minutes, but it keeps someone from calling us pretending to be you.

**Managers and office leads:** please make sure your staff's phone numbers are current with us, and that we have an up-to-date list of your authorized contacts. Your Account Manager can update them.

## What about people without a work phone?

The Microsoft Authenticator app is free and works on personal iPhones and Android phones. It doesn't give your employer or Larkspur any access to your personal phone's photos, messages, or other apps. If someone can't or won't use a personal phone, talk to us. We have options, including small hardware security keys, which we can quote.
