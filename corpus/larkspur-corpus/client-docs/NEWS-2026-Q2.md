## 2. How to write a ticket that gets fixed faster

*By Priya Raman, Service Desk Manager*

Every day our technicians read hundreds of emails and portal tickets. The ones that get fixed fastest have a few things in common. Here's my cheat sheet.

### The five things every great ticket has

1. **Who is affected.** Just you? Your whole team? Everyone in the building? This one detail decides your ticket's priority more than anything else.
2. **What's happening, in plain words.** Describe what you see and what you were trying to do. "When I click Send in Outlook, nothing happens and the message stays in my Outbox" is perfect.
3. **When it started.** And whether anything changed: a new program, a move to a different desk, a power blip, a Windows update last night.
4. **The error message.** A photo from your phone works great. So does a screenshot (press Windows + Shift + S on your keyboard, drag a box, and paste it into your email).
5. **The best way to reach you.** A phone number, and when you're available. If you're about to leave for two hours of meetings, say so, so we don't miss each other.

### Good, better, best

- **Good:** "Printer not working."
- **Better:** "The front desk printer isn't printing."
- **Best:** "The Brother printer at the front desk shows 'offline' on everyone's computer since about 9 AM. The printer screen looks normal. Three of us print checklists from it all day. Call me at the front desk, ext. 101."

The "best" version tells us it's a P2 (several people, an important function), points us to the right device, and gives us a way to reach someone. That ticket will likely be fixed before the "good" one is even triaged.

### Two more tips

- **One issue per ticket.** If your Teams camera doesn't work and you also need a new shared mailbox, send two tickets. They go to different people and get done faster separately.
- **Reply to the same ticket.** When you have more info, reply to our email (keep the ticket number like TCK-123456 in the subject). New emails create new tickets, and your info can get split up.

And remember: **if the business is down, don't write a ticket at all. Call us at (913) 555-0111.** P1 issues must be phoned in.

## 4. Spring cleaning for your computer

A little tidying goes a long way:

- **Clear your desktop.** Files saved on your desktop may not be backed up. Move them into OneDrive or your company's SharePoint so they're protected and available from anywhere.
- **Empty Deleted Items in Outlook.** It frees up mailbox space. If you're getting "mailbox full" warnings, submit a ticket and we can set up an online archive.
- **Unplug what you don't use.** Old USB drives, dongles, and that mystery cable. Fewer devices means fewer driver problems.
- **Wipe down your keyboard and screen.** Turn off the screen first, and use a slightly damp microfiber cloth, never spray directly on the screen.
- **Check your dock.** If your monitors flicker or don't wake up, make sure the dock's power cable is firmly seated. Still flaky? Submit a ticket.

## 5. MFA reminder: never share your number

When you sign in to Microsoft 365, you'll see a two-digit number on your screen and type it into the Authenticator app on your phone. **Only do this when you are signing in yourself.** If your phone asks for a number and you didn't just try to sign in, tap **Deny** and call us.

Larkspur will never call you and ask you to read us an MFA number or code. Neither will Microsoft. Anyone who asks is a scammer. (For a full refresher, see our March 9 MFA announcement.)
