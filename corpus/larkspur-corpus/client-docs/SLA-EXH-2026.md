# Exhibit B to the Master Services Agreement
# Service Level Agreement (SLA)

**Effective January 1, 2026**

This Service Level Agreement ("SLA") is Exhibit B to the Master Services Agreement ("MSA") between Larkspur Ridge IT, LLC ("Larkspur", "we", "us") and the client named in the MSA ("Client", "you"). It describes how we classify support requests, how quickly we respond and work toward resolution, how the SLA clock is measured, and what credits apply if we miss a response target.

Where this Exhibit and the MSA conflict, the MSA controls. Where this Exhibit and any older handout, FAQ, or proposal conflict, this Exhibit controls.

---

## 1. Definitions

- **Business hours:** Monday through Friday, **7:00 AM to 6:00 PM Central Time (CT)**, excluding Larkspur holidays listed in Section 4.
- **Business day:** any day Monday through Friday that is not a Larkspur holiday.
- **Business hour:** one hour of time inside business hours. For example, a P3 ticket submitted at 5:00 PM on a Monday with a 4 business hour response target is due by 10:00 AM Tuesday (1 hour Monday, 3 hours Tuesday starting at 7:00 AM).
- **24x7:** every hour of every day, including nights, weekends, and holidays.
- **Response:** the first response from a Larkspur technician (a real person, not an automatic acknowledgement) by phone, email, portal note, or remote session, in which the technician has begun working on the issue. Automatic ticket confirmation emails do not count as a response.
- **Resolution:** the issue is fixed, or a workaround is in place that lets the affected users get back to work, and the ticket is marked resolved. Some problems need a vendor, parts, or a project to fully fix; in those cases resolution means the business impact has been removed.
- **Ticket:** a record in our service system (Harbor PSA) with a number like TCK-123456.
- **Priority:** the urgency assigned to a ticket using the definitions in Section 2.
- **Managed-services fee:** the monthly per-user fee for your service tier (Essentials, Professional, or Premier), not including add-ons, project work, hardware, licenses, or out-of-scope labor.

## 2. Priority definitions

We assign priority based on business impact. When you call or write in, tell us how many people are affected and whether they can work. The technician who triages your ticket sets the priority and may adjust it as more is learned.

| Priority | Definition | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **P1 Critical** | Business down, or most users unable to work. | Internet outage at the office; server down; email down company-wide; all phones down; ransomware warning on multiple computers |
| **P2 High** | Multiple users affected, or a key business function degraded; or a single VIP/executive fully blocked. | A department cannot reach a shared drive; the main office printer used for all invoicing is down; Wi-Fi down in one wing; the owner cannot log in at all |
| **P3 Normal** | A single user affected; a workaround may exist. | One user's Outlook keeps crashing; one laptop won't connect to the dock; a single user's OneDrive won't sync |
| **P4 Low** | Requests, questions, scheduled changes, and new user setups. | New employee setup; add someone to a shared mailbox; install approved software; "how do I" questions |

If you believe a ticket's priority is wrong, call the support line and ask for the Service Desk Manager, Priya Raman. We would much rather adjust a priority than have you wait.

## 3. Response and resolution targets

### 3.1 Target table

| Priority | Response: Essentials | Response: Professional | Response: Premier | Resolution target (all tiers) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **P1** | 1 hour (business hours only) | 30 minutes (24x7) | 15 minutes (24x7) | 4 hours |
| **P2** | 2 business hours | 1 business hour | 1 hour (24x7) | 8 business hours |
| **P3** | 4 business hours | 4 business hours | 2 business hours | 2 business days |
| **P4** | 1 business day | 1 business day | 1 business day | 5 business days |

### 3.2 Reading the table

- A target marked **24x7** runs around the clock, including nights, weekends, and holidays.
- A target marked **business hours**, **business hour(s)**, or **business day(s)** only runs during business hours.
- The Essentials P1 response target applies to P1 issues reported during business hours. Essentials does not include after-hours coverage. An Essentials client may still call PagerLine after hours; that work is billable at the after-hours rate and is not subject to a response target.
- Professional P1 tickets are covered 24x7. Professional P2, P3, and P4 tickets follow business-hours clocks.
- Premier P1 and P2 tickets are covered 24x7. Premier P3 and P4 tickets follow business-hours clocks (Premier clients may still call for help with any priority after hours, and we will work it, but the measured target runs on business hours).
- The P1 resolution target of 4 hours runs on the same clock as the response target for your tier.

### 3.3 Resolution targets are goals

Resolution targets describe what we aim for and what we report on at your business reviews. Some problems depend on things we do not control, like an internet provider, a hardware warranty part, or a software vendor. **Resolution targets are goals, not guarantees, and are not eligible for SLA credits.**

## 5. How the SLA clock works

### 5.1 When the clock starts

- **Phone:** when your call is answered by a person, or when you leave a voicemail on the support line.
- **Email:** when the email arrives at help@larkspurridge.example and the ticket is created.
- **Portal:** when the ticket is submitted.
- **Monitoring alerts:** when the Beacon RMM alert creates a ticket.

### 5.2 When the clock pauses

The clock pauses:
1. **Outside business hours**, for any priority on your tier that is measured in business hours (see Section 3.2).
2. **While we are waiting on you.** For example: we need you to approve a purchase, a user is not available for a remote session, we have asked for information we need to continue, or the user asked us to call back at a later time. The clock resumes when you reply.
3. **While we are waiting on a third party that you contract with directly**, such as your internet provider, line-of-business software vendor, or printer leasing company, after we have opened a case with them.
4. **During a scheduled maintenance window** that you approved.

### 5.3 When the clock stops

- The response clock stops at the first human response.
- The resolution clock stops when the ticket is marked resolved. If the issue comes back within 5 business days and it is the same problem, we reopen the original ticket and the clock continues from where it stopped.

### 5.4 Priority changes

If priority changes during a ticket, the target for the new priority applies from the time of the change. If a ticket was submitted with too little information to judge priority (for example, an email that says only "computer broken"), the target applies from the time we have enough information to set a priority.

### 6.1 P1 issues must be phoned in

**P1 Critical issues must be reported by phone.** Email and portal submissions are not monitored in real time, especially after hours, and an emailed P1 may not be seen right away. **The P1 response clock for an emailed or portal-submitted P1 does not start until the issue is phoned in.** If your internet is down and you cannot email anyway, call from a cell phone.

### 6.2 What to include

To help us respond quickly, please tell us:
- Your name, company, and a callback number
- What is happening and what you were trying to do
- How many people are affected and whether they can work
- When it started and anything that changed recently
- Any error message (a photo or screenshot is great)

## 7. SLA credits

### 7.1 Credit amount

If we miss a **P1 response target**, you are eligible for a credit of **5% of that month's managed-services fee for each missed P1 response target**, up to a **maximum of 20% of that month's managed-services fee**.

Example: A Professional client with 30 users pays $4,350 per month in managed-services fees (30 x $145). If we miss two P1 response targets in March, the March credit is 10%, or $435.

### 7.2 What is not credited

- Missed resolution targets (all priorities)
- Missed response targets for P2, P3, and P4 tickets
- Add-on fees, project fees, hardware, licenses, and out-of-scope labor are not part of the credit calculation

We still track and report every target, and we review any missed target at your business review.

### 7.3 How to request a credit

Email your Account Manager or billing@larkspurridge.example within 30 days after the end of the month in which the miss occurred, and include the ticket number. We will confirm the miss from the Harbor PSA ticket record and apply the credit to your next invoice. Credits are not paid in cash and do not carry over past the end of your agreement.

### 7.4 Credits are the sole remedy

SLA credits are your sole and exclusive remedy for missed SLA targets, except as otherwise stated in the MSA.

## 8. Exclusions

SLA targets and credits do not apply when the delay or failure is caused by:

1. A P1 issue that was emailed or submitted through the portal and not phoned in (Section 6.1)
2. Issues outside your tier's coverage, including after-hours requests on tiers without after-hours coverage for that priority
3. Outages of third-party services we do not control, such as Microsoft 365 service incidents, internet provider outages, power outages, or a software vendor's cloud service
4. Hardware failures on equipment that is out of warranty or that we do not manage
5. Devices without the Beacon RMM agent, or devices that a user has turned off or disconnected
6. Changes made by you or another vendor without coordinating with us
7. Out-of-scope work, project work, and work on personal devices
8. Delays waiting on information, approvals, or access from you
9. Scheduled maintenance you approved
10. Events beyond reasonable control, including severe weather, natural disasters, widespread internet or utility failures, and acts of government
11. Your account being more than 30 days past due
